# cs255-portfolio

## Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

The DriverPass project focused around designing a system for a company that had the goal of preparing students to take driving tests. They needed a system that would let a student take online practice exams, schedule driving lessons, and manage their accounts. The system also required supporting employees and administrators managing appointments, users, and information.

## What did you do particularly well?

I think I did well when figuring out the different requirements that were required for the system and putting those in a business requirements document. I was also able to use UML diagrams in the system design document to show how the different parts of the system work together.

## If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

I would focus on improving my UML diagrams in my system design document. I feel they show how the system works, but I feel I could add more detail to them to make them that much easier to understand.

## How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the 
## user’s needs when designing?

I interpreted their needs through an interview transcript and identifying what the client wanted the system to do. I used that to help design the features like scheduling driving lessons, taking practice exams, and managing accounts for users. Considering the user's needs is important since the system needs to solve the problems they have.

## How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

When designing software I like to start with trying to figure out what the system needs to do before focusing on the parts to build it. I like to then take what they are trying to acomplish and break it down into smaller parts to help make the design process easier to manage. In the future I would continue to use techniques like gathering requirements, creating UML diagrams, and reviewing those designs to make sure they communicate what the user is needing.
